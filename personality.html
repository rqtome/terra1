Shidou Ryusei (BLLK) Fanpage

Personality

Shidou has a one of the most eccentric personalities in Blue Lock. He has a strong penchant for violence and has an erratic mood, which can shift very quickly and dramatically. This along with his unwillingness to cooperate with people and his complete disregard for camaraderie repels most around him.

He appears fully aware of the toxicity of his behavior and is largely content with it, showing only slight remorse after being electrocuted and locked up for becoming excessively aggressive and threatening serious bodily injury to others. Shido's view of football also makes him highly critical of other players and the ways they use their weapons and approach the game. Because of his own extraordinary ability and overwhelmingly dominant ego, he holds players to an extreme standard of self-expression and becomes frustrated when they fail to fully utilize what makes them unique. His combination of talent, independence, aggression, and ego eventually becomes difficult for even Blue Lock itself to properly handle. His aggressive behavior outside of matches cannot be overlooked, but his ability as a player is equally undeniable. Shido possesses extremely high physical abilities, and every goal he scored within Blue Lock was produced through his own individual skill. His success was not dependent on another player understanding or accommodating him, reinforcing both his self-sufficiency and the difficulty of building a conventional system around him. During the Third Selection, Shido was unable to produce a chemical reaction with Rin even once. Combined with his extremely violent behavior toward Rin at the end of the tryouts, this meant that placing the two together against the Japan U-20 would risk putting the entire team at an overwhelming disadvantage if their conflict continued during the match. For that reason, Ego decided not to place Rin and Shido in the same lineup. Isagi agrees with Ego's reasoning, while Ego adds that, above all else, nobody within Blue Lock is capable of properly handling Shido. Ego reiterates that Shido scored his goals entirely through his own ability and explains that no player within Blue Lock exists who can fully understand Shido's thoughts while simultaneously evolving his level of play. This inability to connect with others is especially important to Shido's character. Unlike players who require a specific partner or tactical structure to bring out their best qualities, Shido can create scoring opportunities through instinct, athleticism, and individual talent alone. However, this independence also creates a ceiling: while he can dominate by himself, reaching an even higher level requires someone capable of understanding his unconventional impulses rather than suppressing them. This makes compatibility with Shido exceptionally rare, as an ideal partner must be talented enough to keep pace with him while also being willing to embrace the unpredictable nature of his football. Shido appears to overcome much of his frustration when Sae Itoshi releases him from his behavioral captivity and asks him to play high-level football alongside him. Under Sae, Shido is effectively permitted to act however he wants as long as his actions serve Sae's purposes. After leaving Blue Lock under Sae's supervision, Shido becomes even more openly aggressive and frequently quarrels with the Japan U-20 team's ace player, Shuto Sendo, who initially does not respect Sae. Sae's influence demonstrates that Shido is not fundamentally incapable of cooperation. Instead, he refuses to cooperate when doing so limits or bores him. Sae succeeds because he does not attempt to restrain Shido's instincts; he understands them well enough to create opportunities that allow Shido to express himself at an even higher level. This gives Shido something he had been unable to find within Blue Lock at the time: another player whose football could stimulate him, challenge him, and expand the possibilities of what he could accomplish. Shido can also be openly friendly and even affectionate toward others. This is particularly apparent with Sae, who gives Shido the opportunity to play alongside him and helps evolve his level of play by exposing him to a style of football he had never experienced before. His positive reactions toward Sae show that Shido's attachment to others is strongly connected to the excitement and freedom they bring to his football. Someone capable of expanding his world can earn genuine admiration from him regardless of his otherwise hostile personality. Shido can also behave in overtly and unnecessarily sexual ways at times, frequently using provocative language to describe the excitement he experiences through football. Despite his tendency toward violence, however, he gradually demonstrates that he is capable of controlling his violent outbursts when doing so allows him to continue playing high-level football for longer. This suggests that his aggression, while extreme, is not entirely uncontrollable; when sufficiently motivated, Shido can restrain himself rather than sacrifice the football experience he values above everything else. Of all the Blue Lock players, Shido appears to have some of the most genuine "fun" while playing football. Losses rarely keep him discouraged for long, and he operates almost entirely through excitement, instinct, and joy while on the field. Whereas many players are driven by fear of failure, revenge, status, or the need to prove themselves, Shido's motivation is primarily the thrill of expressing himself through scoring. His enormous ego combines with this enjoyment to make him relentless, as he does not need external validation to remain engaged in the game. This mentality also makes Shido unusually resistant to setbacks. A failed play or defeat does not permanently undermine his confidence because his identity is not built solely around maintaining an image of superiority. What matters most to him is whether the football being played is stimulating enough to awaken his instincts. As a result, he can remain energized even in difficult situations as long as the match continues to provide opportunities for explosive plays and personal expression. Despite his arrogance and aggression, Shido is able and willing to acknowledge the abilities of other players. During the first match of the Third Selection, he praises Isagi for producing the "best play" of the game, even though Isagi had stolen a goal from him and Rin refused to give Isagi similar recognition. This demonstrates that Shido's ego does not prevent him from appreciating excellence in others. If another player's actions genuinely excite or impress him, he is willing to praise them regardless of whether their success comes at his expense. His willingness to recognize strong play further distinguishes him from competitors whose pride prevents them from acknowledging rivals. Shido is highly self-centered, but he is not necessarily insecure about his own ability. Because he enjoys football itself, another player's brilliance can excite him rather than automatically threaten him. He therefore judges others primarily by the intensity, creativity, and impact of their football rather than by whether recognizing them would damage his own status. By the time of the Neo Egoist League, Shido has developed enough control over his anger and violent tendencies to share the field with his most hated rival, Rin Itoshi, and is even willing to pass the ball to him. Although their hostility has not simply disappeared, Shido's ability to cooperate with Rin represents noticeable growth compared to the Third Selection, when their incompatibility made playing them together too dangerous. This development does not mean Shido has become conventionally disciplined or abandoned his explosive personality. Instead, he has become better at directing those traits toward football rather than allowing them to completely disrupt it. His progression shows that Shido can adapt when adaptation gives him access to stronger opponents, better teammates, and more exciting football. In this sense, his growth is driven by the same principle that defines nearly every aspect of his personality: anything that allows him to experience a greater "explosion" on the field is worth pursuing. Ultimately, Shido is defined by unrestricted self-expression. His violence, eccentricity, instinctive style, physical ability, enormous ego, appreciation for exceptional talent, and obsession with scoring all originate from the same desire to feel completely alive through football. He can be hostile and nearly impossible to manage, yet he can also praise opponents, become attached to players who stimulate his growth, and restrain himself when doing so serves his greater desire to keep playing. Rather than chasing football purely for victory, Shido treats every goal as proof of his existence, making his entire approach to the sport an extension of himself.
